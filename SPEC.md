# Menagerie — Current Specification

## Product

A single-player, phone-first 3D browser game about stacking ceramic animal sculptures. The game runs client-side with TypeScript, Vite, Three.js, and Rapier, and is deployed to GitHub Pages. No accounts, timer, multiplayer, progression, or animal-selection menu.

## Roster and loop

The active roster is tortoise, capybara, toucan, armadillo, ram, and skunk. Only these six models are loaded and offered. Each shuffled bag contains each species once. A fixed tortoise anchors the stack; one incoming animal waits above it in a randomized orientation. A supported, settled placement earns one point. Any released animal reaching the platform or leaving the allowed bounds ends the run, including animals that scored earlier.

## Controls and interface

One continuous rounded-sphere mapping handles every single-pointer drag that begins inside the bubble. A press outside the bubble is never admitted into piece control even if it later crosses the control surface; an accepted piece drag may continue beyond the edge. Central drags tumble the animal; curved drags toward and beyond the sphere's shoulder progressively become twist about the viewing direction. A cubic shoulder removes a hard mode boundary. Rotation is anchored to the pointer-down orientation, so returning to the start undoes the drag and event sampling does not alter the result. The initial pose establishes one physical/control pivot that remains fixed for the entire held phase, including inertial spin. No rotation-driven height correction occurs; the sensor-only lowering path resolves actual clearance after the bubble pops while preserving collider alignment. Each model's full body radius sizes its bubble independently of hover height. Gentle flick momentum is capped at 0.675 rad/s and decays exponentially at rate 5/s; beginning another drag catches it immediately. Position and height are automatic between pieces. A quick stationary tap inside the bubble pops it, clears rotational momentum, and commits the visible orientation after a brief 145 ms visual transition; movement beyond 10 CSS pixels, a press longer than 280 ms, cancellation, blur, or page hiding never places a piece. The bottom control is hidden during play and appears as a circular replay icon immediately on loss. Replay resets the run, score, controls, and camera. There is no dedicated Drop button, top-right restart control, instructional copy, or Game over overlay.

The empty canvas below the bubble is a tower-exploration surface. An 11 CSS-pixel safety moat directly below the bubble remains inert, and presses beside or above the bubble remain inert; exploration begins only below that moat. Horizontal dragging orbits the camera around world-up, limited to 75 degrees on either side of the original view. Vertical dragging uses direct scene manipulation: dragging downward raises the view and dragging upward lowers it, between platform framing and slightly above the current stack. Releasing a deliberate drag carries a short, strongly damped horizontal/vertical glide. The selected orbit becomes the persistent working orientation for piece control and later placements. A temporary height offset holds for 0.5 seconds after momentum settles, then returns smoothly to the live tower-follow height. Touching the exploration surface again catches any glide or height return at its current offsets. The ordinary tower-follow height remains authoritative underneath the temporary height offset, so its return target can rise with the stack instead of becoming stale.

The sphere is softly locked while the camera is moving or has a temporary height offset: it cannot rotate or pop while exploration, momentum, the short height pause, or height return owns the view, and the bubble fades completely out while its logical touch zone remains around the animal. Touching that zone stops orbit momentum and fast-resets height without undoing the selected yaw. Height return has a finite endpoint instead of an asymptotic settling tail. The original 110 ms bubble fade begins during its imperceptible final motion so the bubble is restored as the camera arrives, without a dead pause afterward. If the pointer remains held, the same touch is re-anchored into sphere rotation at that moment; movement during recentering is ignored and that transitional touch can never pop the bubble. Placement, fall tracking, game over, cancellation, blur, and page hiding clear temporary height and motion but preserve the chosen orbit. Replay restores the original orbit. Camera movement and sphere rotation never occur simultaneously.

The default presentation treats the control surface as a playful soap bubble that remains visible for the held phase whenever tower exploration does not own the camera. A subtle celadon interior tint, restrained blue and pink iridescence, bright asymmetric reflections, a lower caustic, and very slow breathing/shimmer animation distinguish it from the pale scene without turning its edge into a heavy control ring. The scene background remains unchanged. Active dragging slightly strengthens the bubble; placement briefly expands and fades it like a pop before the animal begins sensor-only lowering. `?bubble=0` hides the visual for diagnostic comparison; `?bubble=1` explicitly enables it. The same geometric bubble boundary governs input in both diagnostic variants. `?loops=1` gates an experimental rotation-intent overlay: after pointer travel exceeds the same 10 CSS-pixel threshold that disqualifies a tap, three orthogonal great circles fade in, project from the animal's local frame onto the bubble surface, update with its visible orientation, and render near arcs continuously and far arcs faintly/dashed. They fade out immediately on release. Quick taps and stationary hold-to-arrest gestures never reveal the loops, so popping cannot flash them. The ordinary URL leaves these loops disabled, and enabling them does not alter the rounded-arcball mapping. There is no instructional copy, gesture tutorial, or XYZ axis labeling. On page load only, the first animal receives a 4.4 rad/s diagonal flick using ordinary damping; reduced-motion preference skips this cue and removes idle bubble animation. Ordinary replay and subsequent animals receive no opening flick. Cancellation, blur, and page hiding stop rotation without placing a piece.

## Camera

Fixed orthographic zoom with a player-selectable yaw. Normal camera-height tracking rises with the placed tower. Tower exploration changes the persistent working yaw and adds only a temporary height offset; resetting height never corrects the chosen orbit. Only the newest released animal can trigger fall tracking, after 350 ms continuously descending unsupported below the stack top. A confirmed follow stays locked. Downward movement is capped at 2 world units/s and acceleration at 2.5 world units/s², with a slower approach near the target. On loss the camera eases to platform framing without interrupting an existing descent or changing yaw. There is no completion deadline; Play again is available throughout. Replay clears the follow target, timers, camera velocity, temporary height, and selected yaw.

## Physics

Held pieces do not affect the stack. The first held animal uses the fixed presentation position; subsequent bubbles follow the horizontal center of the highest scored animal, capped to 0.65 world units from that default so ordinary tower drift does not become an invisible placement penalty. Placement lowers the sensor-only body to contact before enabling dynamics, preserving the selected orientation. Compound colliders share the visible model's local transform. Rebuilt armadillo, ram, and skunk use geometry-derived convex sections; the skunk's model and colliders share uniform scale 0.9.

Use a fixed timestep and continuous collision detection. Initial landing friction remains low and first-contact damping limits rebounds. After 0.22 seconds of sustained support, the incoming piece advances to moderate settling friction; full stacking friction is still restored only after natural Rapier sleep. Scoring is bookkeeping-only: never force sleep, zero velocities, or change friction on scoring. Settlement accumulates evidence of calm supported contact. Release deadlines and watchdogs prevent unresolved turns; caught engine faults enable replay through a reload.

## Visuals and assets

Broad softened planes, rich ceramic colors, glossy dark eyes, restrained sculpted detail, warm lighting, and readable shadows. Cosmetic blinking, limb animation, and a landing pulse do not alter load-bearing colliders. Original trio: `assets/source/menagerie-lineup-v01.blend`. Armadillo, ram, and skunk: `assets/source/menagerie-reference-rebuild.blend`. Runtime models: `public/models/`.

## Audio

The game ships four approved muted-stoneware contacts generated with Stable Audio 3 Small SFX. The exact normalized audition copies are reused with separate runtime treatments: quiet settling is slightly brighter and softer, ordinary animal contact uses a natural voice, and platform/ground contact is lower and stronger. Audio-file bytes preload independently of the locked Web Audio context. Inside the first user gesture's pointer-down, pointer-up, and click activations, the game creates or resumes one shared context and starts a one-frame silent buffer; its completion confirms the unlock, but a running context may already schedule the first real contact while that confirmation is pending. If WebKit leaves `resume()` pending while the context stays suspended, the next activation in that same physical gesture replaces it synchronously and re-decodes the cached bytes. If the first contact beats buffer decoding, one recent contact is retained for up to one second and played as soon as its decoded family is ready instead of being discarded. On supporting iOS versions the game requests the `playback` audio-session type. Page hiding explicitly suspends the context, while visible/page restoration and the next real gesture attempt recovery from both suspended and interrupted lifecycle states. A visible context whose clock does not advance is treated as stale, then closed and recreated on the next gesture with the cached bytes decoded into the replacement context. Rapier contact-force events are consolidated by rigid-body pair, including `ground:<animal>` pairs, so compound colliders produce one sound rather than a burst. Each pair uses a hysteretic impact-burst detector: after an initial sound, roughly 100 ms of stable force rearms the pair even if physical contact continues, and only a sharp force rise above that recent load can produce a second sound. A short cooldown and a three-trigger-per-second pair budget suppress settling chatter. Measured peak force relative to the moving animal's weight selects the treatment and shapes gain; sample choice rotates across the four variants, with small pitch and screen-space pan variation. `?audio=0` disables audio for automated physics and gesture checks. `?diagnostics=1` exposes non-sensitive DOM state and deterministic species/rotation setup for browser regressions in development and deployed production builds; injected faults remain development-only. `?trace=1` includes audio-context creation, state, unlock, buffer readiness, pending-resume replacement, suspension, recovery, stale-clock replacement, queued/blocked playback, and successful playback events alongside the contact detector data.

`audio/sfx-bank.json` defines the reproducible approved source bank. The generator can also take a separate audition manifest so new material directions do not alter runtime provenance. `npm run audio:generate` preserves raw stereo generations outside the repository, creates mono 44.1 kHz audition copies at a consistent peak level without truncating accepted decay tails, validates their technical properties, rejects candidates with multiple distinct contact onsets, and records model, prompt, seed, processing, and licensing provenance. Only the four explicitly approved processed files are copied into `public/audio/ceramic/`; the audition bundle remains separate from runtime content.

## Validation and delivery

Build with `npm run build`. Browser checks cover all 36 ordered active-species pairs plus tilted placements, ten-second post-resolution observation, input gestures, replay, and failure recovery. Rotation checks exercise bubble-only gesture admission, the inert safety moat, below-bubble tower exploration, direct vertical mapping, bounded persistent orbit, exploration momentum, the short temporary-height pause/return, same-touch fast height recentering, the soft sphere lock, ignored side/outside-to-inside drags, the rounded tumble-to-roll transition, pivot stability, drag-back undo, momentum catching, quick-tap popping, rejection of holds/cancellations, identical results with and without the bubble visual, and URL-gated surface loops that remain inside the bubble, follow orientation, appear only after rotation intent, and stay hidden for quick taps and stationary holds on phone and desktop. Natural falls are valid outcomes; unresolved turns, exceptions, and upward anomalies fail regression checks. Phone feel still requires human playtesting.

An opt-in `?trace=1` recorder captures transforms, velocities, support state, input, scoring, failures, and camera events. Share trace exports JSON; recording survives reload within the tab. GitHub Actions deploys the static build to Pages. Verify the deployed artifact after gameplay changes. Keep build output, credentials, caches, and temporary test artifacts out of Git.
